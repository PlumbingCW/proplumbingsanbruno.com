Westlake, Serramonte, Bayshore and Top of the Hill

Developer Henry Doelger established Westlake in the late 1940s as a major planned residential district, and the tract homes of that era may still carry the original supply and drain lines. Serramonte, home to Serramonte Center, mixes newer apartments and shopping. Bayshore sits on lower ground, and Top of the Hill lies near the older core.

Each area has its own plumbing profile. Older tract homes lead to repiping and drain camera work, while apartment areas lead to shared stack backups and water heater swaps.

A blended water supply

The agency profile says Daly City draws from the San Francisco Regional Water System through 11 connections, with five active municipal wells and recycled water for irrigation. The wells are blended with regional water because some well water exceeds the nitrate limit. The city serves about 98,000 people.

Blended water changes from block to block. We test hardness at your tap if scale looks heavy, and we talk about a water softener only if the results back it up.

Fog, hills and the 1906 earthquake

Fog rolls through Daly City in spring and from early to late summer, and the winters are mild and wet. The 1906 San Francisco earthquake struck just off the coast near Mussel Rock, which is part of why the city grew when it did. Hillside streets mean pressure varies and drains run steep.

We check pressure with a gauge during any supply repair and look at drain slope during camera work. See burst pipe repair for what a fast fix involves.

From the Cow Palace to Lake Merced

Landmarks such as the Cow Palace and Lake Merced sit along the city's northern edge. Businesses near them, and those along Serramonte Boulevard, need after-hours plumbing as much as homes do. We pair our commercial crews with the same 24/7 line.

Daly City meets San Francisco to the north and borders Colma and Brisbane nearby, so we also answer calls from there. South San Francisco sits to the east.

Homes from the Westlake era

Doelger's Westlake development began in the 1940s and filled a large part of the city with rows of similar houses. Homes of that age can still have original galvanized supply lines, early copper or old cast iron drains. Because the houses are so alike, one repair often tells us what to expect in the neighbors.

That is useful to know when you are deciding whether to patch or replace. A single leak in galvanized pipe often means others are coming, and a camera on a slow main drain can show whether the lateral is original. See repiping and sewer inspection for the options.

If you are buying or selling in Daly City, a plumbing inspection before the sale gives both sides a clear picture of what the pipes are doing.
