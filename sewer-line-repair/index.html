Emergency Sewer Line Repair in San Bruno, CA

A sewer backup is a health risk. We answer 24/7, camera the line to find the real problem, and repair it with the least digging that will work.

  • 24/7 emergency line
  • Camera findings shown
  • Lining or replacement

Sewage backing up through a floor drain or tub is the most urgent plumbing call there is. Stop using water, do not flush, and keep everyone away from the backup. We answer 24/7, nights and weekends included, and we start by finding out whether the blockage is a clog or a broken pipe.

Many backups are solved with a cleaning. When the same line backs up again within weeks, the pipe itself is damaged and needs a repair.

Sewer backup: what to do while you wait

  • Turn off the washing machine and stop all showers, dishes and flushing.
  • Keep the backed-up area clear of children, pets and food items.
  • Do not pour chemical cleaner into the drains.
  • Take photos of the backup and any damaged items for insurance.
  • Call 24/7 and tell us which fixtures are affected.

How we find what is wrong with the line

The first step is a camera inspection. A waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels the lateral from the cleanout toward the street. It shows roots, offsets where pipe sections have drifted apart, cracks, bellies where the pipe sags and holds water, and broken sections.

We record the run and show you the footage, so you decide with the evidence in front of you. The same recording helps with the city's lateral certificate on an older home. See sewer camera inspection for the planned version of this service.

What San Bruno owners should know

San Bruno's municipal code Chapter 10.13 covers the point-of-sale sewer lateral requirement. The city says enforcement began on May 8, 2015, and that the rule applies to homes over 50 years old. The city's Public Works Wastewater office runs the program and publishes the certificate forms.

If you are about to list an older house, a camera inspection ahead of time lets you fix problems on your schedule. Before any excavation, call 811 so utility lines are marked. That is standard safety guidance repeated by PG&E on its gas safety pages.

Pipe lining and sewer line repair on older homes

Older laterals in the area may be clay, cast iron or Orangeburg, a bonded fiber pipe that softens with age. Each fails differently. Clay cracks at joints and takes roots. Cast iron rusts through the bottom. Orangeburg deforms and flattens.

Pipe lining suits clay and cast iron when the pipe is still continuous. A flattened Orangeburg line usually needs replacement. We explain which fits your pipe before any work begins.

What affects sewer repair cost

Sewer repairs vary more than most plumbing jobs, so the price is set after the camera inspection.

  • Depth of the pipeA shallow lateral is simple. A deep one needs shoring and more excavation.
  • Length of the damageOne short section costs less than a full replacement.
  • Surface above the pipeConcrete, pavers or a driveway add demolition and restoration.
  • Permit and inspectionWork on the lateral usually involves the city. Ask the Building Division about permit rules for your address.
  • Cleanup and accessWhere the lateral runs under a patio, a tree or a fence changes the plan.

The city's point of sale lateral rule

According to the City of San Bruno, homes over 50 years old need their sewer lateral inspected and certified before sale under Municipal Code Chapter 10.13. The city says enforcement began on May 8, 2015.

A repair done before a sale should be recorded on camera before and after, so the certificate application has what the city asks for.
