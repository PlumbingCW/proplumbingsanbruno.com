Sewer Camera Inspection in San Bruno, CA

A camera shows what is happening inside the lateral. We record the run, mark problem spots and explain what the footage means.

  • Recorded footage
  • Point-of-sale support
  • Repair options explained

A sewer camera inspection is the quickest way to see what is happening inside a lateral you cannot see. A waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels from the cleanout toward the street, and a monitor shows the pipe as the camera moves.

This is a planned service for home purchases, point-of-sale compliance and recurring clogs. If sewage is backing up now, see sewer line repair. We answer emergencies 24/7.

What we look for

FindingWhat it means
Roots entering at jointsSmall leaks at joints are drawing roots into the pipe
Offset or separated jointSections have shifted, catching paper and slowing flow
Belly or sagA low spot holds water and solids
Cracks or breaksSoil may be entering and water escaping
Scale or grease buildupNarrowing of the pipe wall by deposits
Collapsed sectionThe pipe is crushed and needs replacement

The inspection process

  1. Locate the cleanoutWe find the access point, which is often a capped pipe near the house or the property line.
  2. Run the cameraThe camera travels toward the street, and we note the distance of each feature.
  3. Record and markWe record the run and mark the pipe location above ground if a repair is needed.
  4. Review with youWe walk through the footage and explain what matters and what does not.

San Bruno's point-of-sale sewer lateral requirement

The City of San Bruno says that if your home is over 50 years old, your sewer lateral must be inspected and certified by the city before it is sold. The city says enforcement began on May 8, 2015, and the program sits under Public Works Wastewater and San Bruno Municipal Code Chapter 10.13.

The city's page lists a compliance certificate, a time extension certificate, an application, and a link to an outside warranty provider. It does not say who performs the inspection, so call the wastewater office to confirm what it accepts.

A camera run helps even if you are not selling

Many older laterals have a hidden problem long before a backup. A recorded camera run gives you a baseline and a repair plan on your schedule. Waiting means paying for an emergency repair at the worst time.

If the pipe is healthy, you get a recording that shows it. If it is not, you can budget for lining or replacement. For cleaning after the camera shows buildup, see sewer line cleaning.

Older neighborhoods and old materials

San Bruno was incorporated in 1914, and neighborhoods such as Lomita Park were annexed in 1953. Laterals installed across those decades range from clay to cast iron to Orangeburg to plastic. Each material fails differently, and the camera tells us which you have.

Crestmoor, Mills Park and Rollingwood include canyons and ravines, so lateral slopes and depths vary across the city.

What affects sewer inspection cost

Inspection prices depend on access and scope.

  • Cleanout accessA good cleanout makes it quick. A missing one adds work.
  • Line lengthLonger laterals take more time.
  • Cleaning firstA heavily blocked pipe needs cleaning before it can be filmed.
  • ReportingA recording and notes for a transaction take time.
  • SchedulingPlanned visits are scheduled in regular hours.
