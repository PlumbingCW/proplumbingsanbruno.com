2

What hard groundwater does to heaters and fixtures

South San Francisco's water comes from two places. Cal Water's Bayshore District report for the system says it used a combination of local groundwater and surface water purchased from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, with six groundwater wells and one treatment plant. The report lists groundwater hardness from 287 to 461 ppm, averaging 384 ppm, while the purchased water averaged 62 ppm.

By Cal Water's own scale, 300 ppm and above is very hard. That is enough to coat heater elements, clog showerheads and shorten the life of a tank. It is also why water heater replacement and a water softener come up so often in this city.

Neighborhoods and buildings across the city

The city lists about 30 neighborhoods, among them Avalon Park, Brentwood, Buri Buri, Mayfair Village, Sunshine Gardens, Westborough, Winston Manor and Oyster Point. Sign Hill, with its hillside sign listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1996, overlooks the downtown. Orange Memorial Park sits in the middle of the city.

South San Francisco was conceived as an industrial suburb and incorporated in 1908. Genentech opened here in 1976, so the commercial side includes biotech campuses, warehouses and restaurants. For business calls, see commercial plumbing.

Housing mix: owners and renters

The 2020 census counted 66,105 residents and 22,683 housing units, with 56.7 percent owner-occupied and 43.3 percent renter-occupied. That split shapes how repairs happen. Renters call us when a landlord is slow. Owners call us when an older house starts leaking in more than one place.

We explain every finding in plain terms, so tenants can pass details to a landlord and owners can decide. If a line of old pipe keeps failing, we say so, and repiping is one option.
