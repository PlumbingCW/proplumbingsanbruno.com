Plumber in Mills Park, San Bruno, CA

Plumbing help for post-war houses and remodels in Mills Park, next to San Bruno City Park.

Mills Park lies in the flatter part of San Bruno, with San Bruno City Park along its southern edge. The name comes from Darius Mills, the banker who bought much of the old San Bruno land. In the 1940s, a builder named George Williams bought much of the Mills land and built houses there for support personnel and returning veterans, which became the Mills Park Addition.

A listing guide places the neighborhood between Interstates 280 and 380 and Highway 101, though the city does not publish a formal border.

Small post-war houses, many of them changed

A listing guide describes mostly one-story post-war houses of about 1,000 to 1,500 square feet, with three bedrooms and an attached garage. It puts the median year built near 1948 and notes that some houses have been expanded or rebuilt to about 3,000 square feet. These are estimates.

That mix matters for plumbing. A 1940s core with a 2000s addition has two eras of pipe in one building, and a leak can sit at the join. We look at both.

What Mills Park calls often involve

Additions and added baths

New fixtures connect to old drains and supply lines, so flow and venting get checked. See plumbing installation.

Toilets and faucets

Running toilets and worn faucets are common in houses of any age. See toilet repair and faucet repair.

Main line clogs

When more than one drain backs up together, the main line is the likely cause. See sewer line cleaning.

Heater upgrades

Older tanks get swapped during remodels, and some owners move to tankless. See water heater installation.

The sewer lateral rule matters here

A typical Mills Park house is decades past the 50-year line, so sellers here should expect the city's lateral program to come up. The city dates enforcement to May 8, 2015.

If you are planning to list, a camera sewer inspection shows the pipe's condition first. If it needs work, sewer line repair explains the options.

Nearby

Another older, flat neighborhood, Lomita Park, lies along the El Camino Real corridor.

Sources: the San Francisco Peninsula history of San Bruno, Wikipedia, and the City of San Bruno Point of Sale Sewer Lateral page. Median year built, house size and boundaries come from a real estate listing guide and are estimates.
