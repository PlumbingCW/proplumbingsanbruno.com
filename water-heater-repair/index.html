Emergency Water Heater Repair in San Bruno, CA

A leaking or dead water heater should not wait. We answer 24/7, repair gas, electric and hybrid units and tell you honestly when replacement is the better call.

  • 24/7 emergency line
  • Gas, electric, hybrid
  • Same-day repairs

No hot water, a puddle at the base of the tank or a rumbling heater are signs to act on. If water is leaking, shut off the cold water supply valve on top of the tank and turn off power or gas. We answer 24/7 and can often repair the unit the same day.

If you smell gas, do not stay to look at the heater. Leave the home, avoid switches and phones inside, call 911 and then PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. We will be glad to help once the utility says it is safe.

Water heater leaking: what to do first

  1. Turn off the power at the breaker, or set a gas unit's control to pilot or off.
  2. Close the cold water shutoff valve on the pipe feeding the tank.
  3. Attach a hose to the drain valve if the tank is leaking and run it outside or to a drain.
  4. Move belongings away from the puddle and take photos for insurance.
  5. Call the 24/7 line.

A leak from the tank itself cannot be repaired. The steel is gone.

No hot water, lukewarm water and short hot cycles

With no hot water, we first check power or gas. On an electric heater we test the elements and thermostats with a meter. On a gas heater we check the pilot, thermocouple, igniter and gas control valve. A sediment-choked tank may fix itself with a flush if the elements and burner are healthy.

Short hot cycles mean the tank is full of sediment or one element has failed. Lukewarm water suggests a failed upper element, a bad thermostat or a dip tube that has deteriorated.

Hard water and sediment in San Bruno

Hardness shortens the life of water heaters. The city's 2023 water quality report lists local groundwater at 215.6 ppm hardness as calcium carbonate, with a range from 158 to 324 ppm, while San Francisco Regional Water System water averaged 46 ppm. The mix at your tap depends on your street and the season.

Hard water leaves scale on elements and the tank floor. A yearly flush helps, and the anode rod should be checked every few years. If scale is heavy, a water softener can protect the heater.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters

Gas heaters have burners, pilots, vents and gas valves. Electric heaters have elements, thermostats and a high-limit switch. Heat pump hybrids add a compressor and fan, plus a condensate drain.

Each has its own failure points, so we match the parts to the unit. A gas heater that backdrafts needs venting checked at once, because carbon monoxide is dangerous.

Repair or replace?

A tank water heater typically lasts around a decade, though water quality and maintenance change that. If the heater is near that age and leaking, replacement is the answer. If the unit is younger and the problem is a part, repair makes sense.

When the numbers favor replacement, see water heater replacement. If you are weighing a tankless upgrade, tankless water heater service explains the trade-offs.

What affects water heater repair cost

Water heater repairs range from a quick part swap to a decision to replace.

  • Fuel typeGas, electric and hybrid units use different parts.
  • Part and availabilityThermostats and elements are common. Gas control valves and heat pump components cost more.
  • Age and conditionOn an old tank, a repair may not make sense.
  • Access and ventingA unit in a tight closet or attic needs more time.
  • Code itemsStraps, a pressure relief discharge pipe or an expansion tank may need to be corrected.

Earthquake bracing and what the state requires

According to California Health and Safety Code section 19211, water heaters must be braced, anchored or strapped against falling or horizontal movement. We check the straps on every repair visit.

A repair that involves the gas valve or venting may need a permit. The Building Division at 650-616-7058 can confirm.
