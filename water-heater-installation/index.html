Water Heater Installation in San Bruno, CA

A planned install is the best time to fix the layout, venting and straps. We size the unit, handle code items and walk you through the controls.

  • Sizing and venting
  • Seismic straps
  • Permits handled

Installing a water heater is more than setting a tank and connecting pipes. The location, the vent path, the gas or electric supply, the drain, the expansion tank and the earthquake straps all have to fit together. This page covers planned installs, which is a different service from an emergency replacement.

If your current heater has failed and you need hot water today, see water heater replacement. For a planned install, call us during regular hours and we will schedule it.

What a proper water heater installation includes

  1. Plan the locationWe check clearances, venting, combustion air, floor drain access and the route for gas, water and power.
  2. Set and secure the unitThe heater goes on a stand or pan where required and is strapped to the wall studs.
  3. Connect water, fuel and ventingFlexible connectors, shutoff valves, a pressure relief valve and a discharge pipe go in. Gas units get a drip leg and shutoff, and vents are sealed.
  4. Add the expansion tank if neededA closed system needs a tank sized for the heater volume and the water pressure.
  5. Test and walk you through itWe fill, purge air, light or power the unit, check for leaks and combustion safety, and show you the controls.

Code and permit items in San Bruno

San Bruno adopts the 2025 California Plumbing Code under section 11.20.010 of its municipal code, and the building official is the administrative authority. The Building Division at 567 El Camino Real runs the SB Permits online system and can be reached at 650-616-7058. The page lists counter hours of Monday through Thursday, 7:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.

We handle permit steps for our installs. Moving a heater or changing fuel types usually involves more scrutiny than a like-for-like swap, so plan for time.

Seismic bracing

Section 19211 of the California Health and Safety Code says new and replacement water heaters, and all existing residential water heaters, shall be braced, anchored or strapped to resist falling or horizontal displacement due to earthquake motion. San Bruno sits close to the San Andreas Fault, since San Andreas Lake lies just outside the city limits.

That is why we strap every heater to wall studs, and why we check old installs for loose or missing straps even when the unit is not being replaced.

Bay Area air rules and heat pump options

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District adopted a zero-NOx standard for residential water heaters. The district said it applies to new heaters at the time of replacement, not to existing units, and that draft amendments from July 2026 would move the date from January 2027 to January 2028. They were not final on the page we reviewed.

If you are planning an install in the next year or two, it is worth asking about heat pump models, which meet the standard. Ask us what is available.

What affects water heater installation cost

Installations vary more than replacements because each house has a different starting point.

  • EquipmentTank size, efficiency and fuel type change the price.
  • RelocationMoving the heater means new pipe, vent and sometimes electrical work.
  • Venting and gas lineChanging vent type or upsizing a gas line adds labor.
  • Electrical capacityHeat pump and electric units may need a dedicated circuit.
  • Permit and inspectionFees and scheduling add time.

What to do before we arrive for an install

  • Clear a path from the door to the heater and clear the space around it.
  • Find your main water shutoff and the gas shutoff, and tell us where they are.
  • Move stored items away from the heater closet or garage wall.
  • Plan for water to be off for part of the day.
  • Tell us about stairs, tight doors or a crawlspace before the visit.

Permits, bracing and inspections

A new installation is normally permitted, and the inspector checks gas, venting, the relief valve drain and the bracing. According to state law in Health and Safety Code section 19211, the heater must be braced or strapped.

In San Bruno the Building Division handles permits through its SB Permits portal and its counter at 567 El Camino Real.
