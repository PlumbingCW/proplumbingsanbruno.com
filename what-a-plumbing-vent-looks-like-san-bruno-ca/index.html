What a Plumbing Vent Looks Like on a San Bruno, CA Roof

That pipe sticking out of the roof is the vent. Here is how to recognize it, what damage looks like and how rain can make a vent boot look like a leak.

By the San Bruno Pro Plumbing team

A plumbing vent on the roof looks like a plain pipe sticking up through the shingles. Most people never notice it, and some mistake it for a chimney or a bird perch. This guide shows what vents look like, where they are usually found and what signs of damage to watch for.

It is a companion to our general explainer on how the venting system works, and it focuses on what you can see from the yard.

What a plumbing vent looks like

A typical roof vent is a round pipe, usually 2 to 4 inches across, made of PVC, ABS or cast iron. It rises a foot or more above the roof, often painted or weathered gray. A rubber or metal collar around its base is the flashing, which seals the hole in the roof.

Some vents have a small cap or a screen to keep out leaves and animals. Others are open. An open top is normal and necessary, because air has to move through.

Where to find them

  • On the back slope of the roof, away from the street view.
  • Near bathrooms and kitchens, since vents run up from those drains.
  • Often in a line if several bathrooms are stacked.
  • Sometimes near the roof ridge on older houses.
  • In older homes, cast iron vents may pass through the attic.

Signs of roof vent damage

What you seeWhat it may mean
Cracked rubber collarWater can leak into the attic
Rust around the baseFlashing is failing
Stain on a ceiling after rainThe boot is leaking, not a plumbing line
Bent or leaning pipeThe vent may be separated below
Nest or debris at the topThe vent may be partly blocked

Why San Bruno rain matters

San Bruno has a Mediterranean climate with dry summers and wet winters. Most of the rain falls between November and April, so roof vent boots that dried and cracked over the summer face their test in the first storms. A leak that only appears during rain is often a vent boot.

If you see a ceiling stain after a storm, look first at the vent flashing. If the stain appears when a fixture is used, call a plumber for leak detection.

What to do about a damaged boot

A cracked boot can sometimes be replaced without removing shingles. A sealant patch is a short-term fix at best. Replacement boots slide over the vent and under the upper shingles.

Roofers usually handle the flashing. Plumbers handle the pipe. If you are unsure which is the problem, we can inspect the vent below the roof and tell you whether the pipe is sound.

Gurgling drains and the roof

A vent blocked at the top can cause gurgling drains and slow flow. Birds, leaves and debris are the common causes. A plumber can clear the vent with a cable or a hose from the roof. If you hear gurgling, see drain cleaning for how we separate a vent problem from a clog.

A safety note

Keep off the roof when it is wet, windy or steep. Many San Bruno lots sit on slopes, and some roofs are high. Binoculars from the yard give a good look at the vent without a ladder. If the vent needs work, let a professional do it.

What you can check from the ground

You do not need to climb to see most of what matters. Stand back from the house in daylight, with binoculars if you have them, and look along the roof for round pipes sticking up. Plumbing vents are usually a few inches across and sit a short distance above the roofline.

Note whether the pipe is straight, whether it has a cap or screen and whether the base looks cracked or lifted. Compare what you see with the number of bathrooms and kitchens inside. Houses often have two or three vents.

  • Check that the vent pipe is not leaning or broken.
  • Look for a rubber or metal collar at the base, called a boot or flashing.
  • Watch for nests, leaves or debris at the top.
  • Note any rust stains or water marks on the ceiling below.
  • Keep a photo so a plumber can see what you saw.

Boot and flashing leaks

The weak point on most roof vents is the rubber collar around the pipe. Sun and age crack it over time. When it fails, rain runs down the outside of the pipe and shows up as a stain on a ceiling or wall near a bathroom or kitchen.

That leak is a roofing repair, not a plumbing one, but a plumber can tell you whether the vent itself is the cause. If the pipe is cracked or blocked, we can repair that part. See drain cleaning if slow drains accompany the stain.

When the pipe itself is the problem

A vent can fail in ways that are not visible from the ground. The pipe inside the attic can separate at a joint, which lets sewer gas into the house. A cracked section can leak condensation. A blocked section can cause gurgling that comes and goes.

These issues show up as smells, stains in the attic or drains that sound wrong. A plumber can trace the vent from a fixture to the roof and test it with a camera or a smoke test. We describe what we find and show you photos.

Roof vents look much the same in Millbrae and Daly City as they do in San Bruno, and the same ground-level check applies to a home in either town.

San Bruno rain and roofs

Wikipedia's summary of San Bruno's climate describes a Mediterranean pattern with dry summers and cool, wet winters, with fog and low overcast common on summer nights and mornings. It says most precipitation falls from November to April, and that annual precipitation at the San Francisco International Airport weather station is 20.11 inches, with higher hills exceeding 32 inches.

That seasonal pattern is why vent boots that cracked in a dry summer often leak in the first storms. The city's hills, with Crestmoor above 600 feet and Portola Highlands above 700 feet, get more rain than the flats. A roofer or plumber in Pacifica, where annual rain averages about 30 inches, sees the same pattern even more strongly.

Questions about this topic

It depends on the layout. A small house may have one or two. A larger home with several bathrooms may have more. The number is not a measure of quality, and fixtures can share vents.

Some have caps to keep out debris, but a vent should never be completely sealed, because air must move in and out. Ask a plumber if you are not sure.

Vents release sewer gas above the house. A faint smell in calm weather near a vent is normal, but a strong smell inside the home is not.

Roof work is dangerous, particularly on a wet or steep roof. We recommend checking from the ground with binoculars, or having a professional inspect it.
