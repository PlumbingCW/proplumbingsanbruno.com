Repiping in San Bruno, CA

When pinhole leaks keep coming back, replacing the pipe is cheaper than repairing it again. We repipe with PEX or copper and handle the permit.

  • PEX or copper
  • Permit handled
  • Walls patched

Repiping replaces the supply lines inside your walls, floors and crawlspace. It is a planned project, not an emergency, but the pipes it replaces often fail in an emergency first. When pinhole leaks keep returning, repeated repairs cost more than a single replacement.

This page explains the signs, the materials, the process and the local conditions that matter in San Bruno.

Signs your house may need a repipe

  • Pinhole leaks that keep appearing in different spots.
  • Brown, yellow or rusty water from taps, especially after the water sits overnight.
  • Low water pressure that worsens even though the city supply is steady.
  • Corroded, flaking or green-stained pipes and fittings.
  • A house with original galvanized steel supply lines.

Galvanized pipe replacement

Galvanized steel was a common supply line in older homes. It rusts from the inside, narrowing the pipe until pressure drops and water turns brown. Eventually, a fitting or a thin spot gives way.

Once galvanized lines start to fail, the next section is usually close behind. We replace the whole run, since patching one leak does little for the rest.

The repipe process

  1. Inspect and planWe trace your existing lines, locate shutoffs and plan the new route and manifold or branch layout.
  2. Protect and openWe protect floors and furniture and cut access holes in drywall at planned points.
  3. Run new pipeNew lines go in, connected to each fixture, with pressure testing before walls are closed.
  4. Inspect and patchPermit inspection happens, then we patch the openings. Painting and texture are left to you or a contractor.

Housing in San Bruno and why age matters

San Bruno has about 16,622 housing units, and 55.5 percent are owner-occupied according to the 2020 census figures cited on Wikipedia. The city grew after the 1906 earthquake and spread across the decades that followed, so plumbing eras are mixed.

Homes with original galvanized lines are candidates. Homes with 1970s or 1980s copper may be fine. We look at your pipes before recommending anything.

Pressure, hills and your new pipes

A repipe is a good moment to check water pressure and add a regulator if needed. San Bruno's system uses 31 pressure regulating stations to serve elevations from the flats to the foothills, so pressure varies by street.

We measure at a hose bib with a gauge, and if it runs high, a pressure regulator protects the new pipes and your fixtures.

Permits and inspection for a repipe

A repipe is permitted work under the California Plumbing Code, which San Bruno adopts under section 11.20.010. The inspector checks the pipe, the fittings and the pressure test before the walls are closed.

The Building Division accepts permit applications through its SB Permits portal, and its counter is open Monday to Thursday.

Frequently asked questions

Common signs are repeated pinhole leaks, brown or rusty water when you first open a tap, low pressure that gets worse over time, and visible corrosion on pipes in the basement, garage or crawlspace. One leak does not mean a repipe, but a pattern of leaks does.

Most homes take a few days. The work involves opening walls at strategic points, running new lines, connecting fixtures and patching drywall. Water is off for part of each day. Larger or two-story homes take longer.

Both work. PEX is flexible, faster to install and resistant to freezing damage, so it often needs fewer wall openings. Copper is rigid, durable and familiar. We explain which suits your house, your water and your budget.

Repiping a house is a major plumbing change, and permit rules vary, so ask the San Bruno Building Division. Its online system is SB Permits and its phone is 650-616-7058. We handle the permit and inspection steps for work we do.
