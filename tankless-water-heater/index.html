Tankless Water Heater in San Bruno, CA

Endless hot water depends on the gas line, the vent and the water quality. We install, repair and descale tankless units around San Bruno.

  • Install, repair, flush
  • Gas line checked
  • Hard water descaling

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it, so it never runs out when the demand is within its flow rating. It also asks more of the home. Gas line size, venting, a condensate drain and hard water scale all shape how well it works.

We install, repair and flush tankless units. This page covers all three, since the three jobs share the same hardware and the same local water chemistry.

Tankless water heater installation

Installation starts with a load calculation. We total the flow of fixtures you might run at once, then compare it to the unit's output at your incoming water temperature. Cold-water temperature matters, and a unit that handles a hot bath in summer may struggle in winter.

Next comes the gas line. Tankless units often need a larger gas pipe, and the meter must deliver enough volume. Venting follows. Most use stainless or PVC through a wall or roof, and the manufacturer's clearances are strict.

Tankless flush and descale

Scale is the enemy. As water heats in the narrow passages of the heat exchanger, dissolved minerals drop out and coat the metal. The coating insulates the exchanger and raises the temperature required to deliver the same output. Eventually the unit overheats, throws codes or loses flow.

A flush runs a descaling solution through the unit with a small pump, then rinses with clean water. We also clean the inlet screen. In hard water areas, many manufacturers call for yearly service, and your manual is the final word.

Why local water chemistry matters here

The city's 2023 report shows local groundwater at 215.6 ppm hardness, with a range of 158 to 324 ppm. San Francisco Regional Water System water is softer, averaging 46 ppm. The city says groundwater made up about 29 percent of its supply in 2023, so the blend at a particular tap changes.

That variability is the reason we recommend testing your tap instead of guessing. If your water is hard, a water softener or a regular flush schedule protects the heat exchanger.

Tankless water heater repair

The display code is the first clue. Ignition failures point to gas supply, the igniter or flame rod. Overheat codes often mean scale. Flow errors can come from a clogged inlet screen or a failing sensor. Vent errors may mean a blocked termination or a condensate problem.

If the unit is a leaking tankless heater, shut off the water and gas to it and call us. A leak can come from a loose fitting, a failed heat exchanger or a clogged condensate trap. We find the source before recommending repair or replacement.

Venting and exhaust

The vent carries combustion gases out of the home, and mistakes are dangerous. We check for proper slope on condensing vents, sealed joints, clearances from windows and a clear termination. A bird nest or snow drift in a termination will shut a unit down.

If you are replacing a tank with a tankless unit, the old chimney flue usually cannot be reused. A new vent path is part of the plan.

What to do if a tankless unit acts up

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911, then PG&E at 1-800-743-5000.
  • Do not relight or reset the unit repeatedly when an error code returns.
  • Turn off the gas valve and the water valve if the unit leaks.
  • Keep the area around the vent clear of stored items.
  • Call us with the error code you see on the display.

Gas safety and manufacturer guidelines

Venting and gas sizing follow the maker's installation manual and the California Plumbing Code. The manual's guidelines override any rule of thumb.

According to PG&E's gas safety guidance, if you smell gas, leave the area, call 911 and then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. Keep carbon monoxide detectors working in any home with gas appliances.
