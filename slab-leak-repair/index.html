Emergency Slab Leak Repair in San Bruno, CA

A leak under a concrete floor can hide for weeks. We answer 24/7, locate it within inches and explain every repair option before we touch the slab.

A leak under a concrete slab can run unseen for weeks, and once it surfaces it often means a larger problem than a single pipe. If you see water at the base of a wall, feel a hot patch on the floor or hear water running with everything off, shut off the main valve and call. We answer 24/7 and can send a plumber the same day.

Our first job is to confirm that the slab is the source and locate the leak within inches.

Slab leak emergency: signs to act on today

  • A warm or hot area on a floor, especially over a hot water line.
  • The sound of water rushing when every fixture is off.
  • Water meter spinning with everything shut off.
  • Damp carpet, lifted flooring or new cracks in tile.
  • A sudden drop in hot water or low pressure.

How we locate a slab leak

We begin by isolating each line, hot and cold, to see which holds pressure. Then we use acoustic listening gear on the floor and thermal imaging to read heat from a leaking hot line. Together they narrow the leak to a small area.

A pipe locator marks the path of the line so we can open the floor in exactly the right place. That limits the size of the repair and the cost of restoring the surface.

Why slab leaks happen in older homes

Copper under a slab sits in direct contact with concrete and soil. Over decades, chemistry in the soil, abrasion against gravel and vibration from pressure changes can wear thin spots in the pipe. Hot water lines suffer more because heat speeds corrosion.

San Bruno's water supply is a blend. The city's 2023 report shows local groundwater averaging 215.6 ppm hardness, with the rest coming from the San Francisco Regional Water System, which is much softer on average. Water chemistry that changes from street to street is one reason leaks cluster in some homes and not in neighbors.

Pressure, hills and slab lines

The city uses 31 pressure regulating stations across an area that runs from near sea level into the foothills. Houses on lower streets see higher static pressure than houses above them. If your pressure regulator is missing or worn, the slab lines take the force every minute of every day.

We check pressure after a slab repair and recommend a regulator when it is high. It is a cheap part that protects every pipe in the house.

What affects slab leak repair cost

Slab leaks are priced by repair method more than by hour.

  • Location of the leakA leak under a hallway is simpler than one under a kitchen island or a bathroom with built-in cabinets.
  • Floor finishTile, hardwood and carpet each need different restoration.
  • Repair methodSpot repair, reroute and repipe each carry different labor and materials.
  • Pipe conditionIf the pipe is corroded in several places, replacing the line beats repeated repairs.
  • Permits and drywallRerouting involves wall openings, patching and possibly a permit from the Building Division.

What to do if you suspect a slab leak

  • Check the meter with every fixture off, and note whether the dial moves.
  • If you find a hot spot on the floor, keep children and pets off it.
  • Do not dig or drill into the slab to look for the pipe.
  • Close the main shutoff valve if water is surfacing or the floor is wet.
  • Call us and describe the sound, the warm spot or the bill change.

Permits for floor and reroute work

According to the California Plumbing Code, slab repairs that open a floor or reroute lines usually involve a permit, which San Bruno adopts under section 11.20.010 of its municipal code.

The Building Division at 650-616-7058 tells you what applies at your address. If the work reaches outside the house, call 811 before any digging.
