The homes of Millbrae and what is inside the walls

Darius Ogden Mills bought part of Rancho Buri Buri in the 1860s to build a country estate, and the name Millbrae combines his surname with the Scottish word for a hillside. The city incorporated on January 14, 1948 and covers 3.29 square miles, with 8,679 housing units and 60.4 percent owner-occupied.

A house in a city that incorporated in the late 1940s is more likely to have galvanized steel or early copper supply lines and cast iron drains than modern PEX. Those materials are where pinholes, rust and root-infiltrated joints start. A camera and a pressure test tell us which problem you have.

Supply lines

Millbrae water: a single source

The City of Millbrae owns and operates its own water utility. The Bay Area Water Supply and Conservation Agency profile says the San Francisco Regional Water System is its only source, delivered through five turnouts, with 22,087 people served over 3.2 square miles and 6,613 accounts. The profile also lists a connection to Burlingame as its only intertie.

San Bruno's 2023 report puts the average hardness of that regional water at 46 ppm, which is soft compared with local groundwater. That means scale is a smaller problem here than in cities with their own wells, and leaks and sewer lines come first on our list.

Downtown, the station and small businesses

The downtown runs along El Camino Real and Broadway Avenue, with small shops and restaurants. The Millbrae station is a hub for BART, Caltrain and SamTrans, and Green Hills Country Club, built in 1929, forms a green belt in the center of the city.

Restaurants and cafes near the station build grease in their drains, and a shared line in an older strip building can back up into a neighbor's unit. See commercial plumbing and drain cleaning for what we do.
